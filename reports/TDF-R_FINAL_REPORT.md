# TDF-R Final Report

*Compiled 2026-08-31. Covers Phases 13-18 of this mission: the reasoning-
optimizer audit, protected-information regression suite, the additive §7
reporting layer, semantic-tree detection, and the v2 accuracy re-run
(`openai/gpt-oss-120b`, budgets 512/1024/2048/4096, 1 seed, 263 questions x
10 arms x 4 budgets = 10,520 attempted completions, 9,771 completed). Prior
phases (0-12) are covered in `validation/`, `reports/BASELINE.md`,
`reports/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md`, and `eval/results/REPORT.md` (v1) — this
report does not repeat them, it builds on them and updates what changed.

Raw data: `eval/results/raw_v2_{512,1024,2048,4096}.jsonl`,
`eval/results/v2_analysis.json`, generated by `eval/results/analyze_v2.py`.
v1's data is preserved untouched at `eval/results/archive_v1_256tok/`.

---

## Does Tree + Language Compression improve TDF?

**Compression: yes, and it replicates.** TDF-family arms save 42-44% of
prompt tokens vs Markdown on this corpus (`tdf_full` 43.1%, `tdf_hoist`
44.1%, `tdf_nocaret`/`tdf_nocodes`/`tdf_nodict` all within a point of
`tdf_full`) — inside the mission's 30-50% target band, and consistent with
v1's independently-measured 43.1% (same number, different run, same
corpus). `toon` saves 38.6%; `hybrid` 41.3%; `json` is **89.6% larger**
than Markdown on this corpus, not smaller.

**Accuracy: still negative vs Markdown, at every budget level tested.**

| Budget | tdf_full vs md | 95% CI | md accuracy | tdf_full accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | -8.7pp | [-14.3, -3.2] | 51.4% | 43.5% |
| 1024 | -10.5pp | [-16.0, -5.0] | 50.0% | 39.6% |
| 2048 | -12.2pp | [-17.7, -6.8] | 48.8% | 36.2% |
| 4096 | -13.6pp | [-19.1, -7.7] | 45.8% | 32.1% |

Every CI's upper bound stays negative at every budget — under
`eval/PREREGISTRATION.md`'s decision rules, this is squarely "the format
costs real accuracy" territory (CI upper bound < -4pp) at 1024/2048/4096,
and borderline-marginal at 512. **Language + structural compression
measurably helps token cost and measurably hurts QA accuracy on this
corpus, at every budget tested.** Neither claim should be reported without
the other.

## Where does it help?

- **Dictionary phrase resolution** (`deref_dict`): TDF wins outright —
  100% at budget=2048 vs md's 0%. This replicates v1's finding
  (`deref_dict` 66.7% vs 33.3%) and is now even starker.
- **Column association**: near-parity with md (93% vs 93% at budget=2048
  for `tdf_nocaret0`; 85-93% across the TDF family) — the `!C`/`!V`
  columnar structure is not the problem.
- **Token efficiency** on structured/tabular content, as above — this was
  never in question and remains true.

## Where does it hurt?

- **Row association** is the dominant, reproduced weak point. At
  budget=2048: md 88%, `tdf_full` 49%, `tdf_nocaret0` 52%. This is the
  *exact* Phase-5 failure pattern (caret-eliding a lookup key removes the
  row's identity anchor from the wire) — still present in v2, and the
  targeted fix for it (`tdf_nocaret0`) recovers only 3pp of the 39pp gap.
- **Cross-reference** resolution: 0% for `tdf_full`/`tdf_nocaret0` at
  budget=2048 vs md/toon's 50% — but this qtype has only **n=2** per arm at
  this budget (one right, one wrong for md/toon; both wrong for
  `tdf_full`/`tdf_nocaret0`). That is not a usable sample; one flipped
  answer swings the percentage 50pp. The same md/toon=1-of-2,
  tdf_full/tdf_nocaret0=0-of-2 split repeats at budgets 512 and 4096 too
  (1024 has n=1 for every arm and everything reads 0%, uninformative), so
  there's a consistent *direction* worth re-checking once the corpus has
  more cross_reference questions — but `json` breaks the pattern at 4096
  (1/2, matching md/toon), so this is a lead, not a confirmed finding.
- **Exact identifiers**: TDF is not uniformly worse here (`tdf_full` 75%
  actually beats md's 50% at budget=2048) but the pattern is noisy across
  budgets, not a clean win.
- **Multi-hop table reasoning**: md 57% vs `tdf_full` 27%, `tdf_nocaret0`
  20% at budget=2048 — a substantial, reproduced gap.

## How many tokens does it save?

42-44% vs Markdown for the TDF family (see table above); 38.6% for `toon`;
`json` costs more tokens than it saves. These numbers are budget-
independent (prompt token count doesn't change with the completion budget)
and were computed once per arm across all 5 corpus documents at 512's run
(prompt tokens are identical across budget levels since only
`EVAL_MAX_TOKENS` — the completion cap — differs).

## Does LLM reasoning improve or degrade?

**Degrades, and — this is the unexpected finding of this re-run — it
degrades *further* as completion budget increases, for every arm except
`json`/`toon`.** `tdf_full` goes 43.5% -> 39.6% -> 36.2% -> 32.1% as budget
rises 512 -> 4096; `md` itself declines too, just less steeply (51.4% ->
45.8%, a 5.6pp drop vs `tdf_full`'s 11.4pp drop over the same range).

This directly contradicts the hypothesis v1's README committed to: that
inadequate completion budget (256 tokens, 73% cap-hit) was suppressing
accuracy, and a bigger budget would recover it. It does not recover it —
per-arm accuracy monotonically *falls* as budget rises from 512 to 4096.

**What we can and can't conclude about why:**

- Cap-hit rate (completions landing at/near the budget ceiling — see
  Limitations for how this is measured and why it's a proxy, not ground
  truth) *does* fall sharply and monotonically with budget: roughly
  53% -> 31% -> 17% -> 5% across the four levels, averaged over arms. So
  truncation is genuinely less frequent at higher budgets — confirmed, not
  just assumed.
- Despite that, accuracy still falls. So whatever recovers when truncation
  clears up is not enough to outweigh whatever gets worse as completions
  get longer. This report does not have a confirmed mechanism for the
  latter — plausible candidates (untested here): the reasoning model
  "overthinking" into a worse final answer when given more room; the
  strict-match scorer penalizing longer, more hedged completions on
  types like `negation`/`numeric_comparison` that already score in the
  single digits at every budget; or non-determinism in the provider's
  serving stack across different `max_tokens` configurations even at
  `temperature=0, seed=1` (NVIDIA's `seed` parameter is best-effort, not a
  documented guarantee, and this run used real concurrent load).
- **This finding should not be read as "TDF is fine, budget doesn't
  matter."** The accuracy gap vs Markdown is present and mostly stable in
  sign and rough magnitude at every budget (-8.7 to -13.6pp) — the
  budget-vs-accuracy inversion is a separate, additional finding about the
  eval methodology and/or the model, not a resolution of the format
  question.

## Which transformations are safe? Which should be disabled?

Unchanged from v1's ablation-ladder conclusion, and now cross-checked
across 4 budgets instead of 1: no single mechanism removal recovers enough
accuracy to justify disabling it by the pre-registered ≥3pp threshold.
`tdf_nodict`, `tdf_nocodes`, `tdf_nocaret` all track within a few points of
`tdf_full` at every budget — the accuracy cost is a property of the format
overall (density, unfamiliarity to the model), not concentrated in one
removable mechanism. This matches v1's "individual mechanisms are not the
problem" finding and extends it: it holds at four different completion
budgets, not just the confounded 256-token one.

**One new, previously-unmeasured mechanism now has data:
`tdf_nocaret0`** (Phase-5's row-anchor fix, audited in
`validation/reasoning_optimizer_audit.md` recommendation #2 as unshipped
and unmeasured before this run):

| Budget | tdf_nocaret0 vs md | tdf_full vs md | Δ (nocaret0 - full) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | -10.2pp | -8.7pp | -1.5pp |
| 1024 | -12.4pp | -10.5pp | -1.9pp |
| 2048 | -9.7pp | -12.2pp | +2.5pp |
| 4096 | -10.3pp | -13.6pp | +3.3pp |

**Verdict: inconclusive, do not ship.** The sign flips between budgets —
the fix looks slightly *worse* than plain `tdf_full` at 512/1024 and
slightly *better* at 2048/4096. With 1 seed and ~230-255 paired
observations per cell, this is exactly the kind of small, noisy delta that
needs more seeds before it's trustworthy either direction. Recommendation
#2's original plan (ship if the data supports it) is updated: **the data
does not yet support shipping it.** The next accuracy run should include
`tdf_nocaret0` across 3 seeds, matching v1's methodology, before this
question is revisited.

## Is query-aware compression worthwhile?

Not evaluated — still correctly out of scope per `research/
opportunities.md`'s own deferral reasoning (thinner differentiation until
the base accuracy question settles). This run adds evidence that it
*hasn't* settled favorably, which if anything reinforces waiting.

## What should TDF-R become next?

1. **Capture `finish_reason` from the API, not just content.** The single
   biggest methodological gap this run exposed: cap-hit is currently a
   token-count proxy against a mismatched tokenizer (see Limitations). One
   field addition to `eval/runner/client.py` would replace a proxy with
   ground truth for every future run.
2. **Re-run `tdf_nocaret0` at 3 seeds before deciding whether to ship it.**
   The mechanism most likely to move the row_association number is also
   the one with the least reliable data behind it right now.
3. **Investigate the budget-vs-accuracy inversion directly** — it's a
   bigger open question than anything about TDF specifically. A same-
   prompt, repeated-sampling experiment (multiple completions at the same
   budget) would separate "more budget hurts" from "this provider isn't
   deterministic under load," which this run cannot distinguish.
4. **Row association and cross-reference remain the real target for the
   next compression change**, not new token-savings mechanisms — every
   ablation confirms the accuracy cost isn't concentrated in a removable
   mechanism, so the fix has to be structural (the semantic-tree wire
   encoding scoped in `tdf/tree.py`, still detection-only, is the most
   direct lever available).
5. **Widen the corpus's question distribution.** `sec_filing` still
   supplies the large majority of questions (matching v1's caveat); several
   qtype cells above (`cross_reference` especially) have single-digit n per
   arm per budget and should not be over-interpreted until the Phase 6
   corpus expansion (12 new documents, still with zero questions written
   against it) gets its own question set.

## Limitations of this run (read before citing any number above)

- **1 seed, not 3.** v1 used 3 seeds (n≈789/arm); this run trades seeds for
  budget levels (n≈230-260 paired/arm/budget) to make four full grids
  finish in a single background run (~10 hours actual, vs an original
  ~7-hour estimate — see below). Every CI here is correspondingly wider
  than v1's, and single-seed accuracy numbers carry more sampling noise.
- **Cap-hit rate is a proxy, not measured ground truth.** `completion_tokens`
  is recounted locally with `tiktoken`'s `o200k_base`, but the server
  enforces `max_tokens` against `gpt-oss-120b`'s own tokenizer. An exact
  `completion_tokens >= budget` check read a flat, implausible 0% at every
  budget; budget=512's actual completion-token percentiles (p50=p90=p99=509)
  make clear responses WERE landing right at the ceiling, just 1-3 tokens
  short of it by this recount. `analyze_v2.py` uses a `budget - 5` tolerance
  band instead — better, but still an estimate, not the API's own
  `finish_reason`.
- **Corpus is unchanged from v1's 5 documents**, dominated by `sec_filing`
  (see per-qtype small-n warning above). The Phase 6 corpus expansion
  (12 new documents across 7 families) has no questions written against it
  yet and contributed nothing to this run.
- **Real elapsed time was ~10 hours, not the ~7-hour estimate** given when
  this run was launched — budget=512 alone took ~5h47m against a projected
  ~2h, apparently due to endpoint load variance over the run's early hours
  (later budgets ran faster: 1024 took 1h39m, 4096 took 48m). Concurrency
  (15) and timeout/retry settings (240s, 3 attempts) were tuned before
  launch and produced an acceptable 2-9% skip rate (rising with budget,
  as expected — larger completions are slower and more likely to exhaust
  retries) rather than the ~10% skip rate an earlier, un-fixed version of
  the client hit in testing (see the client.py timeout-retry fix, commit
  `df29dfd`).
- **`tdf_nocaret0` is measured for the first time in this run, ever** — v1's
  raw.jsonl has zero rows for it (registered as an arm but never actually
  run). Treat its numbers as a first look, not a confirmed result.
